How it works

From a match to a written agreement

Most tools stop at introductions. PartnerPortal takes you from finding an organization whose needs line up with yours through to a summary both sides have actually agreed to.

1

2

4

Two-way matching

Every organization is scored on what it offers you and what you offer it. Mutual matches rank above one-sided ones.

Structured needs and offers

Both sides pick from the same 33 categories, so a match is a real overlap rather than two descriptions that happen to share a word.

Agreements, not just intros

Propose what each side will provide. Once both confirm, you get a summary page you can send to a board or a funder.

What makes it different

Built around what each side brings, not just what they want

A partnership where only one side gains is a favour, and favours run out. Matching on both directions is the whole design, from the categories an organization picks during onboarding through to the terms of the final agreement.

Why this exists

Small organizations already have what each other needs

A nonprofit with a hall standing empty and no one to run its outreach. A studio down the road with time to donate and no cause to donate it to. They rarely find each other, and when they do it is usually by accident.

PartnerPortal started because a nonprofit I know lost a partner organization and had no way to find another one like it. Everything here is built around that problem: making the trade explicit, so both sides can see what they are getting before they commit.
